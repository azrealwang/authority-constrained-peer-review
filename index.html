01 / The position

Review judgments should earn
their decision influence.

Peer review both evaluates research and allocates scarce academic opportunities. Yet novelty, significance and other consequential judgments often lack reliable ground truth at the time of a decision.

Our position shifts the institutional focus toward governing the authority of these judgments. Independent scrutiny should make their influence conditional and give evaluators reasons to make consequential claims more carefully.

Read the foundational paper ↗
Our guiding question
What must a review judgment withstand before it can shape a decision?

A governance principle for human, AI and hybrid review systems.

01

Separate critique from authority

Writing a substantive critique should not automatically grant its author unilateral control over a consequential score or recommendation.

02

Require independent scrutiny

Check the clarity, evidential support and interpretation of consequential judgments before they acquire full decision relevance.

03

Keep judgments contestable

A challenge must be able to lead to clarification, revision, downgrading or removal of a judgment from the decision rationale.

04

Account for unsupported influence

Preserve the record when a judgment shapes a decision and is subsequently found to be poorly supported, including how its authority was corrected.
